# Barua design principles

## A quiet place for letters

Barua exists to celebrate long-form, heartfelt letter writing. It should get
out of the writer's way and leave room for the pleasure, patience, and thought
that a letter requires.

Barua is not an attention product. It should not use streaks, badges,
progress pressure, artificial urgency, engagement loops, or repeated nudges to
keep people returning. Someone who opens Barua should be there because they
want to write or read a letter.

## Constraints should be clear and quiet

The app may have rules that protect the character of correspondence, but they
should be simple and explained once. They are boundaries of the medium, not
challenges for the user to overcome.

The initial minimum length for a letter is 100 words. This is a hard send rule,
not a target, score, or progress indicator. Barua shows a quiet word count for
orientation, but does not encourage users to add filler; a shorter thought can
wait until it has become a letter.

Drafts are different from posted letters: they are useful working material, not
part of Barua's correspondence archive. Drafts are encrypted and kept only on
the current device and browser. They are restored after an interruption,
removed after sending, and never uploaded to the Barua server.

## Privacy supports the experience

Privacy remains a serious engineering responsibility, but it should not make
ordinary letter writing feel like operating security software. The normal path
through the app should be calm and familiar, with recovery and technical
details available when they are actually needed.
